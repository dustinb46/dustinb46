01 · Trade

The USMCA review clock just struck — and dairy's two biggest export markets are on the table

The first mandatory Joint Review of USMCA was due to launch by July 1, and U.S. dairy groups are pushing to make Canada's under-filled tariff-rate quotas and Mexico's stalled "common name" rules central issues; the pact can be extended 16 years or reopened. Why it matters: more than 40% of U.S. dairy exports by value go to Mexico and Canada, so the outcome sets the demand floor under milk prices — and a contentious review injects trade uncertainty into the back half of 2026.

02 · Milk Price

New Zealand's milk keeps flooding — Fonterra closes its biggest season in a decade

Fonterra collected 1.5708 billion kg of milk solids in the 2025/26 season, up 4.1% year-on-year and its highest in ten years, with May the strongest May on record on favorable pasture. Why it matters: a record flush from the world's largest dairy exporter keeps global powder and cheese supply heavy, capping the export prices that feed directly into U.S. Class III and Class IV — a headwind to hold against any domestic price rally.

03 · Herd Economics

The milking herd is near a 30-year high, but the replacement pipeline behind it is empty

USDA's January count put the share of dairy heifers expected to calve at a record-low 26.1%, and CoBank projects replacements entering the herd will shrink further before rebounding in 2027 — with the average dairy-replacement cow already fetching about $3,130/head this spring as beef-on-dairy breeding diverts would-be heifers. Why it matters: record-priced, hard-to-find replacements change the math on culling, expansion and how long to keep marginal cows in the string — every open stall now carries a four-figure refill cost.

04 · Demand & Basis

A wave of new cheese plants is about to collide with a milk supply that isn't growing fast enough

Leprino's Lubbock, Texas plant is nearing full capacity this summer at up to 8 million lb of milk a day, part of a build-out that also includes Daisy Brand's $600M Boone, Iowa facility (≈43,000 cows' worth of milk daily) and Bel's Brookings, S.D. expansion. Why it matters: new processing capacity coming online faster than the cow herd is a bullish pull on farm-level milk demand and component basis in the plants' draw areas — but it also intensifies competition for a milk supply the heifer shortage is capping.

05 · Animal Health

New World screwworm is creeping north as USDA races to stand up domestic sterile-fly production

Confirmed U.S. screwworm cases — the first since eradication in the 1960s — have climbed through June across Texas and New Mexico counties, while USDA's new sterile-fly production plant at Moore Air Base in Edinburg, Texas (broke ground in April) isn't slated to reach an initial 100 million flies a week until late 2026. Why it matters: until domestic sterile-fly capacity comes online, any fresh wound, calving site or dehorning is a screwworm risk in the spread zone — a reason to tighten wound management and inspection now, even well north of the current front.

06 · Feed

Forecasters flag a strong El Niño building into 2027 — a feed-price wildcard worth hedging early

The GEOGLAM Crop Monitor warns of a roughly 63% chance the developing El Niño reaches strong-to-very-strong intensity between November and January, stressing crops in Southern Africa, Central America, India and Australia even as it tends to bring wetter conditions to the southern U.S. Why it matters: El Niño's biggest hits fall on overseas grain and oilseed regions, so it's a global feed-supply risk that could firm corn and soybean-meal prices into 2027 regardless of the U.S. crop — a variable to weigh when timing forward feed coverage.

07 · Science & Tech

The methane additive has a milk-check catch: peer-reviewed data show a small yield drag

Across pooled studies, the feed additive 3-nitrooxypropanol (Bovaer) cuts enteric methane by roughly a third, but at a cost — energy-corrected milk yield falls about 0.8 kg/cow/day (≈2%), with the drag larger in early lactation. Why it matters: as processors and sustainability programs start dangling premiums or requirements for methane-reducing additives, the modest production penalty is a real line item to price into any enrollment — the carbon math and the milk-check math don't automatically agree.
