The 48-Hour Read for Dairy Operators

Daily Edition · Friday, July 24, 2026

Supply is running ahead of demand: June milk output rose 2.4% and component solids climbed faster still, even as processors trim capacity and global buyers reward powders over cheese. On the risk side, two animal-health items — field tests of a genetically engineered screwworm fly and an FDA push on domestic veterinary-drug supply — are worth a herd manager's attention.

01 · Milk Supply

June milk output up 2.4% as the national herd keeps growing

USDA's latest Milk Production report puts June 2026 output about 2.4% above a year earlier, driven by both more milk per cow and a larger milk-cow herd — a continuation, not a one-month blip, of the 2026 expansion. Why it matters: more milk chasing the same processing capacity is the single biggest weight on the milk check into the second half, and it frames every hedging and expansion decision this quarter.

02 · Processing

A Wisconsin cheese maker pulls two plants offline, cutting 97 jobs

Prairie Farms is ending operations at its Shullsburg creamery and the nearby White Hill Cheese plant in southwest Wisconsin, eliminating 97 jobs by late August, in what the cooperative called a routine evaluation of its manufacturing network. Why it matters: when a cooperative rationalizes cheese capacity in the heart of dairy country, it signals margin pressure in Class III processing and can reshuffle where area producers' milk gets hauled and priced.

03 · Global Markets

Global auction rebounds — but powders and cheddar split hard

The July 21 Global Dairy Trade auction rose 1.5%, its first gain in a month, on strength in milk powders — whole milk powder up 1.6% and skim up 2.8% — while butter slipped 0.6% and cheddar fell 6.5%. Why it matters: a market that's bidding up powders while dumping cheddar points demand toward Class IV over Class III, a useful read for anyone weighing which class to hedge into the fall.

04 · Animal Health

Genetically engineered screwworm flies head into their first outdoor test

Scientists at a U.S.-funded facility in Panama are preparing the first outdoor trials of NovoFly, a screwworm engineered to produce only (sterilizable) males, as a next-generation weapon against a flesh-eating parasite that has pushed north through Central America; officials have pegged potential losses from a U.S. outbreak at over $1 billion to Texas livestock alone. Why it matters: New World screwworm is a direct threat to cattle, and how fast an eradication tool matures bears on herd biosecurity and any future movement or treatment rules.

05 · Inputs

FDA moves to shore up the domestic supply of animal drugs

The FDA's Center for Veterinary Medicine launched a pilot that fast-tracks reviews for animal drugs whose finished product and active ingredient are both made in the U.S., and lets sponsors name a second domestic ingredient source up front. Why it matters: veterinary-drug shortages hit herd health directly, and prioritizing U.S.-made supply is aimed squarely at the supply-chain gaps that leave dairies short of key medications.

06 · Global Trade

Another foreign player exits China's dairy aisle

Japan's Meiji agreed to sell its Chinese drinking-milk, yogurt and B2B businesses to Shanghai AustAsia Food for about RMB 320 million (~$47.2 million), citing a tougher, more competitive China market and redirecting resources toward chocolate; the deal is slated to close at year-end. Why it matters: China is the swing buyer for world dairy, and continued retreat and consolidation there shapes the import demand that ultimately backstops U.S. export prices.

07 · Components

The volume number understates it: component-adjusted milk grew 3.1% in May

The latest Dairy Market Report notes that on a component-adjusted basis — accounting for rising butterfat and protein — May milk production grew about 3.1%, outpacing the raw volume figure as solids-per-hundredweight keep climbing. Why it matters: processors and Class III/IV formulas pay for pounds of solids, not fluid volume, so component gains quietly add to effective supply and reward herds shipping high-component milk even when the headline volume looks softer.
