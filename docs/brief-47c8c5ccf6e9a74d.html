01 · Trade

U.S. exports set up as the release valve for record milkfat and protein

USDA's Foreign Agricultural Service, in its July World Markets and Trade update, projects 2026 U.S. butter exports near 525,000 metric tons and cheese exports above 620,000 tons (about 1.37 billion pounds) as domestic milk production runs roughly 1.2% higher — casting exports as the essential outlet for rising milkfat and protein output. Why it matters: with output at records, foreign demand is what keeps butter and cheese inventories — and their prices — from backing up onto the domestic market.

03 · Animal Health

Idaho is now the epicenter of H5N1 in dairy cattle

USDA APHIS tracking shows Idaho has become the most active state for H5N1 in dairy herds, with dozens of new detections in the past 30 days and spread now driven cow-to-cow through shared equipment and crews rather than from wild birds; California, once the national leader, has cleared its dairies from quarantine since February. Why it matters: the shift to equipment- and personnel-borne spread makes shared-crew and shared-equipment biosecurity — not just bird exclusion — the decisive control point against milk loss and movement restrictions.

August Class I base price drops the most in a year

USDA's Federal Milk Marketing Order announcement set the August advanced Class I base price at $18.76/cwt — down $2.57 from July, the first monthly decline of more than $2 in 2026 — with Class I prices projected to average about $22.88/cwt across all orders once zone differentials are added. 06 · Inputs

Alfalfa growers are underwater — a quiet risk to your forage supply

The American Farm Bureau Federation reports alfalfa prices have fallen more than 40% from their 2021–22 peak, pushing estimated 2025 grower losses to roughly $2.9 billion (about $203 per acre), as China export demand weakens and the crop sits largely outside federal safety-net programs. Why it matters: cheap hay is a short-term win for dairy rations, but sustained grower losses thin high-quality alfalfa acreage and set up sharper forage-price swings ahead.
