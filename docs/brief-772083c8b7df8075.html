01 · Animal Health & Biotech

A CRISPR sterile-fly shortcut enters the screwworm fight as U.S. cases climb

San Diego biotech Agragene was selected for both a USDA New World Screwworm "Grand Challenge" award and a Foundation for Food & Agriculture Research grant to advance a CRISPR-based sterile-male fly (its "pgSIT" platform, with NC State's Max Scott), engineering flies that are born sterile without the irradiation that weakens today's released males. It lands as APHIS-confirmed U.S. screwworm cases reached 32 — nearly all in Texas cattle, sheep and goats — since the first detection in a Zavala County calf on June 3. Why it matters: stronger sterile males mean fewer flies needed per acre to push the outbreak back south; if the parasite keeps moving north it threatens cattle movement, inspection burden and open-wound risk (calving, dehorning, branding) across the herd.

02 · Labor & Policy

The H-2A rewrite is now a bill — and this one drops the "seasonal" wall for dairy

What changed since last week's committee maneuvering: House Ag Chair GT Thompson formally introduced the bipartisan Securing Agriculture's Workforce Act, which would let year-round operations like dairy use H-2A for the first time by removing the program's seasonal-work requirement, and revamp the wage formula and application process. It carries National Milk Producers Federation backing and 400-plus farm groups — the first statutory H-2A overhaul in roughly 40 years. Why it matters: year-round H-2A access is the single biggest potential change to a dairy's labor cost and legal exposure in a generation — but it is a bill, not law, so the near-term value is watching committee markup and the wage-rate language, which decides whether it actually pencils.

03 · Input Costs

USDA puts $500M behind domestic fertilizer — applications close Aug. 15

On July 1, USDA launched the Fertilizer Investment & Expansion for Long-Term Domestic Supply (FIELDS) program, offering $15M–$150M grants through Rural Development to build or expand U.S. fertilizer plants, with applications due via Grants.gov by August 15. The push comes as nitrogen prices climb on Middle East supply disruption. Why it matters: fertilizer is a top-three cost line for the corn and forage that feed the herd; more domestic capacity is a multi-year hedge against the import-price spikes that have whipsawed feed budgets — but nothing about it lowers this season's fertilizer bill.

04 · Trade

The USMCA review lapsed on July 1 — and Canadian dairy quotas are on the U.S. list

At the mandatory six-year joint review, the U.S. declined to grant the 16-year extension Canada and Mexico wanted, tipping the pact into a decade of annual reviews toward a possible 2036 expiration; the agreement stays in force meanwhile. USTR's statement names Canada's allocation of dairy tariff-rate quotas as an outstanding U.S. grievance. Why it matters: roughly a sixth of U.S. milk solids move as exports, and this both keeps Canadian market-access leverage alive and injects years of trade uncertainty into export planning and forward milk-price expectations.

05 · Genetics & Feed Crops

A seed-genetics startup poaches Syngenta's global seed chief to scale "boosted breeding"

Ohalo Genetics named Justin Wolfe — former President of Global Seeds at Syngenta — as President and COO to commercialize its Boosted Breeding platform, which passes a parent plant's full genome (not a random half) to offspring for faster, more predictable trait stacking, starting with true potato seed and moving into corn. Why it matters: corn silage and grain are the ration's backbone; a breeding method that claims 20%+ yield gains and lower disease risk in feed crops is an early signal worth tracking for future silage genetics and seed-cost math — years out, but the talent move says it's being taken seriously.

06 · Markets

June class prices slid from May's highs — and the depool trap is easing

USDA's monthly class-price announcement shows both Class III and Class IV retreating from their May peaks, with Class IV down more than $2/cwt. That narrows the roughly $5.40/cwt Class III–IV spread seen in May — a gap wide enough to make handlers depool Class IV milk out of Federal Orders, which leaks value out of the blend price. Why it matters: a closing spread means less depooling risk and a more reliable mailbox check near-term, but the softer top-line resets forward margin math — a reason to re-check DRP/DMC coverage rather than assume May's strength holds.
