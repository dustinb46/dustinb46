01 · Animal Health

Texas asks the FDA to put screwworm treatment in the feed bunk, not just the needle

Texas Agriculture Commissioner Sid Miller petitioned the FDA on July 1–2 for an emergency authorization to add ivermectin to medicated feed and mineral supplements for cattle, arguing the recently cleared injectable form can't realistically dose whole herds — this as USDA's New World screwworm count climbed to roughly 29 U.S. animals across Texas and New Mexico and the southern border stayed closed to live-cattle trade. Why it matters: a feed- or mineral-delivered antiparasitic would change how an operation protects cattle against an NWS incursion, but it also puts milk and cull-cow residue-withdrawal compliance squarely on the table.

02 · Beef Channel

USDA puts up to $500M behind small beef processors — and your cull cows ride along

On June 30, Secretary Rollins launched the Strengthening Processing for U.S. Ranchers (SPUR) program, offering up to $500 million in payments to U.S.-owned beef plants that aren't among the dominant "big four," to offset the high cost of buying cattle while the national herd sits near a multi-decade low. Why it matters: with the beef check now driving 12–20% of revenue on many dairies through cull cows and beef-on-dairy calves, keeping independent processors solvent preserves competition and marketing options for dairy-origin beef.

03 · Feed & Inputs

Washington moves on fertilizer costs — a phosphate duty cut and a new $500M grant

USDA announced on June 29 that the administration is temporarily suspending certain countervailing duties on phosphate fertilizer imported from Morocco as "emergency relief," paired with a new $500 million FIELDS grant program for shovel-ready domestic fertilizer projects (applications due Aug. 15). Why it matters: fertilizer is a top-three cost for the corn and forage that set dairy feed budgets, and cheaper phosphate plus new domestic capacity could ease input pressure heading into the next crop cycle.

04 · Crop Protection

Bayer wants tariffs on Chinese glyphosate — up to 446% — and growers are furious

On June 30, Bayer's Monsanto subsidiary — the only U.S. glyphosate manufacturer, making roughly 60% of domestic supply — asked the Commerce Department and ITC for antidumping duties of 68.9% to 446.47% on Chinese-made glyphosate, drawing immediate pushback from corn and soybean groups warning of higher weed-control costs. Why it matters: glyphosate underpins no-till and reduced-till forage and feed-grain programs, so a tariff-driven price spike flows straight into the cost of growing dairy feed.

05 · Food Safety

A soft-cheese Listeria outbreak turns into an all-products dairy recall

A multistate Listeria outbreak tied to requeson-style soft cheese has now sickened 12 people across four states, with 10 hospitalized and one death — and Clover Hill Dairy expanded its recall on June 18 to cover every cheese product made at its facility, followed by a June 26 recall from La Ceiba Foods. Why it matters: when an outbreak triggers a whole-facility recall rather than a single-lot pull, it signals the kind of environmental contamination finding that reshapes processor sanitation audits and liability exposure across the supply chain.

06 · Labeling

"Fresh" milk that isn't: Lactalis pays penalties over reconstituted-powder labels

Australia's ACCC announced on July 2 that Lactalis paid $59,400 over three infringement notices, alleging its Golden North "Country Fresh" and Ferguson Valley "WA Dairy Fresh" 2-litre milks were marketed as "fresh" despite substantial added reconstituted skim-milk and lactose; Lactalis has since dropped "fresh" from both. Why it matters: it's a concrete regulatory marker on how far processors can stretch "fresh" and reconstituted-ingredient claims — the kind of precedent that migrates across markets and reshapes fluid-milk marketing.

07 · Trade

USMCA left unrenewed — and dairy exporters see an opening on Canada

The administration declined on July 1 to renew USMCA in its current form, starting a mandatory annual-review clock rather than a clean extension; U.S. dairy groups, who argue Canada has "flagrantly disregarded" its tariff-rate-quota commitments, are treating the review as leverage to force real quota access. Why it matters: Canada and Mexico anchor U.S. dairy export demand, so both the near-term uncertainty and any renegotiated Canadian access are direct swing factors for milk-price support.
