Dairy Intelligence Brief

The 48-Hour Read for Dairy Operators

Daily Edition · Friday, July 31, 2026

Today's throughline is supply. USDA's July cattle count and June production report both show a dairy herd that is expanding, and the agency has marked its 2026 milk price down to match. Spot butter and powder are already sliding, while animal-health, trade and weather risks sit on the cost side of the ledger.

01 · Herd

USDA's July count shows the milking herd growing, not shrinking

The July 1 Cattle report put all U.S. cattle at 94.2 million head — the first July increase since 2018 — with milk cows up 2% year over year to 9.65 million and milk replacement heifers up 3% to 3.60 million. Why it matters: A bigger cow herd and a deeper heifer pipeline point to sustained milk output and softer farmgate prices into 2027 — the opposite of the tightening many expected from the broader beef cycle.

02 · Milk Supply

June milk output up 2.4%, and more cows are doing the work

U.S. milk production reached 18.93 billion pounds in June, up 2.4% from a year earlier, on 9.23 million cows across the 24 major states — 185,000 more than June 2025 — with output per cow up 7 pounds to 2,051. Why it matters: Herd growth, not just per-cow gains, is now driving supply, which keeps processors long on milk and caps any near-term price recovery.

03 · Margins

USDA marks down the 2026 milk price

In its latest outlook USDA lowered the 2026 all-milk price to $20.70 per hundredweight, down 55 cents from its May forecast, and trimmed Class III to $16.60 and Class IV to $19.35, citing rising cow numbers and per-cow yields. Why it matters: The official price deck is the number your lender and risk-management desk plan around — hedge coverage on second-half milk looks more valuable, not less.

04 · Milk Price

Spot butter slides to a multi-year low as the wholesale complex softens

The USDA National Dairy Products Sales Report for the week ending July 25 had butter averaging $1.60/lb (down 4.6¢), nonfat dry milk $1.63 (down 1.5¢) and dry whey 65.1¢ (down 0.2¢); only 40-pound cheddar blocks firmed, up 3.5¢ to $1.52. Why it matters: Three of the four commodities that set your Class III and Class IV checks are moving lower at once — spot markets are already confirming the outlook's price cut.

05 · Animal Health

H5N1 slows in Idaho, but the national herd tally keeps climbing

New H5N1 detections in Idaho dairies have cooled — nine confirmed in the prior 30 days, the most recent on July 13 — even as Idaho remains the hardest-hit state at 173 affected premises within a national total near 1,166 herds across 19 states since 2024. Officials attribute much of the current spread to shared equipment and crews moving between farms rather than wild birds. Why it matters: A cattle-to-cattle vector means biosecurity on shared trucks, hoof trimmers and relief milkers — not just bird control — is what protects your bulk tank and your production.

06 · Processing

A milk-quality fight goes public between a co-op and a state regulator

A major cooperative announced that early-spoilage problems at a Billings, Montana fluid-milk plant were resolved, but the Montana Department of Livestock said its July 29 inspection — which dismantled equipment and found sanitation and equipment non-compliance — shows the investigation is still open. No recall was issued; testing found no harmful pathogens. Why it matters: When a processor and its regulator disagree in public over plant sanitation and shelf life, it raises the odds of tighter inspections across fluid plants — and reputational risk that can ripple back to member farms.

07 · Trade

New Section 301 "forced-labor" tariffs hit 60 trading partners

Effective July 24, the U.S. Trade Representative imposed additional 10–12.5% Section 301 tariffs on imports from 60 economies — covering the large majority of U.S. imports, including China — citing failures to curb goods made with forced labor. Why it matters: Broad tariffs invite retaliation on U.S. farm exports just as dairy leans harder on foreign sales, and can nudge input and packaging costs — watch for any dairy-specific counter-tariff.

08 · Feed & Inputs

Corn and soybean conditions slip in the heat

USDA rated both corn and soybeans 63% good-to-excellent as of July 26, each down three points on the week, with 78% of corn silking as hot, dry weather is forecast to persist into early August. Why it matters: The crop is entering its yield-defining weeks under stress; a sustained downgrade would lift new-crop corn, silage and byproduct costs right as milk prices soften — a double squeeze on margins.
