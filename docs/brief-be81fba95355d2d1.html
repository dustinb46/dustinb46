01 · Labor

Year-round H-2A is back on the table — the seasonal wall just came down in a new House bill

House Ag Chair GT Thompson introduced the Securing Agriculture's Workforce Act, and unlike the earlier effort we flagged, it explicitly scraps the rule that H-2A work must be seasonal — the single provision that has locked most dairies out of the program — while rewriting the wage formula and streamlining applications. It arrives backed by more than 400 groups, including the National Milk Producers Federation. Why it matters: if it moves, dairies could finally hire H-2A workers year-round rather than lean on an undocumented workforce, and the wage-methodology change would land directly on your labor-cost line.

02 · Feed & Inputs

USDA puts $500M behind domestic fertilizer — but the money flows to plants, not farm gates

USDA launched the FIELDS program on July 1, offering grants of $15–150 million (with a required 50% private match) to build or expand U.S. fertilizer production; applications close August 15. Why it matters: fertilizer is the top input cost squeezing 2026 crop and forage budgets — more domestic capacity could blunt future price spikes, but the relief is indirect and the awards favor large, shovel-ready projects, not near-term costs at the farm.

03 · Margins

USDA's own numbers set up a 2026 squeeze: record milk, softer prices, only modest feed relief

The latest WASDE trimmed the 2026 Class III forecast to $16.60/cwt and Class IV to $19.35 while raising projected milk output to a record 236.4 billion pounds — even as feed eases only marginally, with new-crop corn at $4.40/bu and soybean meal near $310/ton. Why it matters: more milk chasing lower class prices, with feed barely cheaper, is the textbook margin-compression setup — worth stress-testing your DMC coverage and forward contracts before the July WASDE lands.

04 · Herd Health

H5N1 didn't leave — it quietly kept spreading through spring

USDA's livestock tracker shows cumulative H5N1 dairy detections have passed 1,000 herds across 17-plus states, and 2026 brought fresh clusters — a run of Idaho and Texas dairies confirmed this spring after a quieter winter. In hit herds the virus targets the udder, driving necrotizing mastitis and milk "crashing across the string." Why it matters: a single introduction can pull roughly a fifth of a herd clinical and permanently cut yield in survivors — the resurgence is a reminder to tighten cattle-movement and vehicle biosecurity even where cases feel far away.

05 · Manure & Energy

A California dairy's manure just became certified jet fuel — a first, and a new outlet for digester gas

Startup Circularity Fuels ran a six-month pilot converting raw biogas straight from a 5,000-cow Madera, California digester into drop-in jet fuel meeting the ASTM D7566 spec, clearing the CO₂-conversion hurdle that has long made biogas-to-fuel uneconomic; a commercial plant is slated for 2027. Why it matters: if it scales, sustainable-aviation-fuel demand would compete with pipeline RNG and electricity for your digester gas — potentially a higher-value home for manure than today's RNG/RIN pathways.

06 · Competition

FDA clears the first animal-free casein for U.S. sale — aimed squarely at cheese

Berlin-based Formo received an FDA "no questions" letter (GRAS notice GRN 1312) for precision-fermented casein — the milk protein that gives cheese its melt, stretch and structure — clearing it to be sold to U.S. food manufacturers. Why it matters: whey knockoffs have circulated for years, but casein is the heart of cheese; a cow-free, lactose-free version cleared for market is the first real ingredient-side challenge to the cheese demand that has been propping up Class III.

The number that sets your July fluid check: Class I mover slips $0.84

USDA's advanced pricing puts the July Class I base skim milk price at $15.91/cwt, down $0.84 from June, as butter and powder values softened heading into the month. Why it matters: that's the floor for fluid milk shipped this month — a signal the front end is cooling even while cheese holds firmer, widening the gap between fluid and component returns.
