01 · Animal Health

Bird flu roars back in Western dairy herds after a quiet winter

A resurgence of H5N1 is hitting dairies in the Mountain West: USDA APHIS data show Idaho logged more than 30 dairy-cattle detections in the past 30 days and Utah has added several this month, ending a lull in which only about a dozen detections were reported nationwide between mid-2025 and this spring. Why it matters: fresh spread means milk-drop losses, movement restrictions and bulk-tank testing obligations — and signals the outbreak's center of gravity has shifted to herds that never saw 2024's wave.

02 · Milk Price

The powder spike has unwound — global and domestic prices both rolled over

Two weeks after CME nonfat dry milk posted its biggest one-day jump in a decade, powder has reversed hard: at the July 7 Global Dairy Trade auction skim milk powder fell 7.0% and whole milk powder 4.4%, while CME spot NDM has drifted back toward $1.475 from its late-June spike near $1.70. Why it matters: powder's round trip flows straight into Class IV and reprices any Q4 Class IV or DRP hedges placed during the spike — the production-disruption premium is bleeding out.

03 · Exports

US dairy exports topped $900 million in May as cheese and powder volumes surged

US dairy export value surpassed $900 million in May on record nonfat/skim milk powder shipments, a rebound in cheese volume, and recovering whey sales to China, per USDEC's read of the latest trade data. Why it matters: export demand is the pressure valve for a domestic milk glut — every extra ton of powder and cheese moving offshore is what has propped up farm-level prices against surging production.

04 · Trade

USMCA enters its first joint review "not renewed" — with dairy a central grievance

The first USMCA joint review opened July 1 with the US declining to renew the pact in its current form — starting a review clock rather than terminating the deal — and NMPF and USDEC used the moment to press Canada's "flagrant" disregard of its dairy tariff-rate quotas and to demand protection of common cheese names in Mexico against EU geographical-indication claims. Why it matters: Mexico and Canada take more than 40% of US dairy exports by value, so uncertainty over the framework sits directly on top of the export demand now holding up milk checks.

05 · Finance

Italy's oldest dairy just borrowed against cheese still aging on the shelf

Brazzale, founded in 1784, raised roughly €10 million from state lender CDP and Cherry Bank in Italy's first "destocking" securitization — moving about 10% of its maturing cheese inventory into a special-purpose vehicle that borrows against the stock itself, enabled by a 2025 change to Italy's SME securitization law. Why it matters: aging inventory is dead capital for months; turning maturing product into a financeable asset is a template any long-aged-cheese operation could eventually use to free up working capital.

06 · Regulation

USDA and SBA open a joint channel against "lawfare" — aimed at environmental suits

On July 2 USDA signed an MOU with the SBA's Office of the National Ombudsman to jointly triage complaints about federal regulatory and enforcement actions through USDA's "Lawfare Portal" — which has drawn 600-plus submissions from 47 states since April — and to mine that data for deregulation targets across EPA, DOI and DOJ. Why it matters: dairies are frequent targets of Clean Water Act and nutrient-related citizen suits, and a formal federal complaint channel is a new, low-cost avenue to push back on environmental enforcement.

07 · Inputs

USDA puts $500M behind domestic fertilizer — a lever on your feed-crop costs

Secretary Rollins launched the $500 million FIELDS program July 1 to fund construction and expansion of US fertilizer plants, with awards of $15M–$150M for shovel-ready projects and applications due August 15. Why it matters: fertilizer is a top input cost for the corn and forage that set dairy feed prices, so added domestic nitrogen and phosphate capacity is a multi-year hedge against the import-driven price spikes that have been pushing feed costs up.

08 · Processing

Saputo pours C$180M into a Wisconsin whey plant, chasing the protein premium

Saputo has upgraded its Ripon, Wisconsin plant at a cost of roughly C$180 million, lifting WPC80 whey-protein-concentrate output about 35% and adding capacity for higher protein fractions and dry-blend lactose — part of a deliberate pivot from commodity whey toward high-value protein streams. Why it matters: processors bidding up high-protein milk to feed WPC and isolate lines is what rewards herds shipping high-component milk, and it shows the whey-protein crunch pulling real capital into filtration and drying rather than commodity cheese.
