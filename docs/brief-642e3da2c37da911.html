01 · Milk Markets

June class prices snap the spring rally — and the III–IV gap keeps depooling alive

USDA's July 1 announcement set the June Class III price at $15.98/cwt (down 94¢, and $2.84 under a year ago) and Class IV at $20.96 (down $1.36), leaving a $4.98 spread between them. Why it matters: that gap is wide enough to keep processors depooling higher-value Class IV milk out of the order, which can quietly pull down producer blend checks even while the headline numbers still look healthy — the flip side of last week's nonfat-powder spike.

02 · Policy

Unions and counties ask a judge to freeze USDA's reorganization

On July 2 a 31-plaintiff coalition led by Democracy Forward moved for a preliminary injunction to halt USDA's restructuring, arguing Congress explicitly barred the department from using FY2026 funds to downsize or relocate staff without approval; the plan reaches the Farm Production and Conservation mission area — which houses the Farm Service Agency — along with ARS and ERS. Why it matters: FSA county offices process the farm loans, DMC sign-ups and conservation payments dairies depend on, and a rushed relocation that triggers mass departures could slow all of it through the second half of the year.

03 · Regulatory

An Oregon ballot measure to outlaw slaughter clears its signature bar

Backers of Initiative Petition 28, the "PEACE Act," submitted 142,784 signatures — above the 117,173 required — tentatively qualifying it for Oregon's Nov. 3 ballot; as drafted it would strip the legal exemptions that permit slaughter and could reclassify routine breeding and artificial insemination as offenses. Why it matters: if it passed it would effectively bar commercial animal agriculture in the state, and win or lose it is a template activists in other states will copy — worth tracking well beyond Oregon.

04 · Animal Health

Texas wants ivermectin in the feed bunk to fight screwworm

With New World screwworm pushing north, Texas Ag Commissioner Sid Miller formally asked the FDA — on behalf of the Texas Grain and Feed Association — for an emergency authorization to put ivermectin into medicated feed and mineral, arguing the recently cleared injectable version can't realistically protect whole herds. Why it matters: no ivermectin-medicated feed is approved for cattle today, so an authorization would open a fast, low-labor way to protect large herds — and signals how seriously the screwworm threat is now being taken across the cattle belt.

05 · Companies

Bright Dairy backstops Synlait with a fresh loan as its refinance lands

New Zealand processor Synlait finalized a NZ$320 million bank refinancing and drew a replacement NZ$130 million shareholder loan from majority owner Bright Dairy in early July, resetting covenants (shareholder funds must stay above NZ$450 million) and shifting its balance date to line up with its Chinese parent's calendar year. Why it matters: Synlait is a2 Milk's key manufacturer and a bellwether for infant-formula and ingredient supply into Asia — a China-backed rescue keeps that capacity online and shifts control of a large slice of premium milk processing.

06 · Technology

A UK co-op puts satellite nutrient-mapping on all 700 of its farms

First Milk is rolling out a freshwater-protection program across its entire 700-farm membership, pairing two years of nutrient footprinting with satellite mapping to pinpoint where nitrogen and phosphate loss overlaps with landscape risk — and reports early evidence that low-footprint farms match the milk output of high-footprint ones. Why it matters: it's a working, at-scale model for the nutrient-and-water scrutiny already heading toward U.S. dairies, and a data point that tighter nutrient management need not cost production.

07 · Policy

A Senate bill would set a milk floor price tied to cost of production

The Milk from Family Dairies Act, led by Sens. Peter Welch and Bernie Sanders, would create a USDA-run Dairy Market Stabilization Program with production bases and floor prices pegged to farmers' cost of production; it now carries endorsements from more than three dozen farm and dairy groups. Why it matters: supply-management-style floors would be the biggest change to U.S. milk pricing in a generation — long odds in this Congress, but a clear marker of where the small- and mid-herd coalition is pushing.

08 · Compliance

Australia fines Lactalis for calling reconstituted milk "fresh"

The ACCC fined Lactalis AU$59,400, paid July 2, after finding its Golden North "Country Fresh" and Ferguson Valley "WA Dairy Fresh" two-liter milks were sold as fresh while containing reconstituted skim milk, lactose or both; Lactalis has since dropped "fresh" from the labels. Why it matters: regulators are scrutinizing the "fresh" and provenance claims that processors — and the farms whose milk carries them — increasingly market on, a reminder that label language itself is now an enforcement target.

09 · Weather

Europe's heat is pulling milk output down 15–20% in France

During the late-June heatwave — France's most extreme since national records began in 1947 — dairies in western France reported milk production falling 15% to 20% as cows cut feed intake and crowded under barn fans, with analysts flagging Belgium, France and Germany as the hardest-hit milksheds. Why it matters: a concrete read on what a multi-day heat event does to output, and a nudge on the return from cooling, water and heat-abatement investment as these events grow more frequent on both sides of the Atlantic.
