01 · Processing

Foremost Farms to shutter its Marshfield cheese plant — 68 jobs gone by mid-December

Foremost Farms USA filed a WARN notice Aug. 24 confirming it will permanently close its Marshfield, Wis., plant on or around Dec. 15, eliminating all 68 union-represented jobs. The plant makes 40-pound block cheddar, Colby, Monterey Jack, Gouda and condensed whey; the cooperative cited "changing dairy market dynamics," including increased U.S. cheese production capacity and shifting consumer demand. Why it matters: the same capacity boom filling new mega-plants is closing older regional ones — milk in affected sheds gets re-routed, hauling lengthens, and local basis weakens.

Cheese exports run 24% ahead — on pace for a third straight annual record

U.S. cheese exports grew 24% year over year in the first half of 2026, putting the year on track to exceed 700,000 metric tons after records of 510,270 MT in 2024 and 612,671 MT in 2025, per U.S. Dairy Export Council data — with Mexico up 32%, South Korea up 36% and Southeast Asia up 55%, and growth actually accelerating in Q2. 05 · Animal Health

H5N1 is stirring again just as fall migration approaches

After a quiet stretch, APHIS confirmed new H5N1 outbreaks in late August: a 29,700-bird commercial breeding operation in Edmunds County, S.D., a 2,800-bird flock in Brule County, and two more detections in Idaho's Ada and Canyon counties — five flocks nationally in 30 days. Confirmed dairy-herd infections stand at 85 for 2026 to date, down from 171 in all of 2025 and 917 in 2024. Why it matters: renewed detections on the doorstep of wild-bird migration season are the cue to re-tighten biosecurity now — open water sources, shared equipment and poultry-adjacent sites drove the earlier cattle waves.

06 · Risk Management

Cull-cow price insurance now stretches to 52 weeks — while cull checks are at records

USDA's expanded Livestock Risk Protection, effective July 1, lets producers insure cull cow prices for up to 52 weeks (previously 13), widens the insurable weight range up to 1,800 pounds, and carries premium subsidies of roughly 35–40% depending on coverage level — an upgrade drawing fresh trade-press attention as cull values ride the record beef market. Why it matters: cull and beef-cross income has quietly become one of dairy's biggest revenue lines; a 52-week window makes locking in today's historically high cull prices practical for full-year planning, not just next quarter.
